7,000 BC - Rice Beer in Ancient China.

January 16, 2026

In ancient China, people began making fermented beverages using rice, honey, and various fruits. This was one of the first instances where a beer-like drink was created from rice instead of traditional grains—a completely different cultural approach to fermentation.

This beverage was not used for daily consumption. On the contrary, it held special significance. Rice beer was consumed during rituals, religious ceremonies, and royal feasts. It was symbolically connected to ancestors, nature, and the spiritual world.

Rituals and a Global Phenomenon

Here, beer was no longer just food or a source of calories. It became an integral part of culture, tradition, and social identity. Fermentation was perceived as a natural and almost mystical process that connected humans with the order of the universe.

Particularly interesting is the fact that ancient vessels discovered in China still preserve chemical traces of fermentation. These artifacts reveal that the history of beer is not only connected with Western civilization—it is a global phenomenon that emerged independently across different cultures.
