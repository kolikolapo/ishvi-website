Beer in Ancient Egypt.

January 22, 2026

In ancient Egypt, beer was not just a drink. It was food, wages, and an integral part of the culture. Egyptians consumed beer daily - both as a source of energy and as a safe liquid.

During the construction of the pyramids, workers received several liters of beer a day. It was part of their compensation and simultaneously an energy-boosting food. The fermentation process protected the beer from bacteria and made it much safer for health than plain water.

A Shared Beverage of Pharaohs and Workers

Beer did not exist only for workers. It was used in medicinal tinctures, rituals, and festivals. It united society - from the poor to the rich, from the craftsman to the priest.

Even pharaohs drank beer. At the royal court, beer was considered a symbol of strength, fertility, and order. When the same beverage is consumed by both a worker and a pharaoh, it means that beer is already at the heart of civilization, in everyday life, labor, and power.
