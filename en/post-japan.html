The Beginning of Beer in Japan - Meiji Era (1868–1912).

March 16, 2026

The history of beer in Japan begins relatively late - in the 19th century, during the so-called Meiji Era. This was a period when Japan opened up to Western culture and technology. It was this turning point that introduced a completely new drink to the country - beer.

Before that, the main alcoholic beverage for the Japanese was sake, a traditional rice-based drink that had been part of rituals, holidays, and daily life for centuries. Beer was perceived as a foreign, unknown product associated with Western culture.

The history of Japanese beer begins in Yokohama - the city that became the first harbor for Western culture. The first major commercial steps were taken by American and Dutch entrepreneurs, who combined local resources with European knowledge. Initially, beer was brewed only for foreign settlements and remained something of an exotic mystery to Japanese society.

William Copeland, an American of Norwegian descent, opened the Spring Valley Brewery in Yokohama. This was the place where the fusion of local ingredients and Western technology first began. Copeland's legacy is so great that today, one of Japan's largest beer brands (Kirin) is built upon the foundation of that very brewery.

Over time, the Japanese began to taste beer for themselves. Western technologies, production standards, and new forms of flavors were gradually integrated into Japanese culture. This is where Japanese beer began its own path - moving from foreign influence to local interpretation. Interestingly, after the Meiji reforms, many former samurai who had lost their social status tried their hand at business, including beer production. Their discipline and attention to detail perfectly matched the technological requirements of brewing. During this period, beer became not just a drink, but a symbol of Japan's industrial modernization.

The Meiji Era introduced beer to Japan as a symbol of progress, openness, and change. It became one of the most visible examples of connecting with the Western world - a beverage that laid the foundation for a completely new culture.
