1516 - Reinheitsgebot - The Beer Purity Law.

February 23, 2026

At the beginning of the 16th century, a historical turning point occurred in Bavaria. A law was passed that changed the future of beer forever - Reinheitsgebot, the Beer Purity Law.

According to the law, beer had to be made using only three ingredients: water, malt, and hops. Everything else was excluded. This rule served one main goal: quality. Consumers had to be certain that what they were drinking was pure, safe, and stable.

Interestingly, the fourth most important component - yeast - was not mentioned in the original law at all. In that era, microbiology did not yet exist, so fermentation was considered a natural, spontaneous event that happened "by itself." Only centuries later, Louis Pasteur's discoveries proved that yeast is the living organism that transforms the mixture of malt and hops into real beer.

The Reinheitsgebot turned brewing from a craft into a strict standard. Producers became obligated to follow rules, improve processes, and treat each batch with responsibility. Beer was no longer an endless field of experiments - it became a controlled culture.

Violating the law was strictly punished. If someone brewed beer with forbidden ingredients, the court had the right to confiscate the entire batch from the producer and pour it out without any compensation. This strictness forced producers to pay maximum attention to the cleanliness of the process, which ultimately formed the global reputation of German beer.

This is where the modern concept of beer quality begins. The Reinheitsgebot is still considered one of the oldest beverage regulations in the world and a symbol that beer can be made with simple ingredients but with the highest responsibility. This law still reminds us that real craft beer starts where there is no compromise on quality.
