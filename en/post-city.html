17th Century - Beer in the heart of cities.

March 2, 2026

In the 17th century, European cities grew rapidly, and with them, the role of beer changed. Beer moved from homes and monasteries to the city streets - into taverns, breweries, and public spaces.

Taverns and breweries became the center of social life. Craftsmen, merchants, travelers, and thinkers gathered here. Beer was a common language that united people of different classes, professions, and views.

The Tavern — As an Information Center

Beer was part of conversations, the exchange of ideas, and cultural life. The 17th-century tavern was not just a place to drink; it served as a modern information center. The first newspapers were read here, news from distant countries spread, and the fate of the city was decided. Everyone was equal over a mug of beer - both the craftsman and the nobleman, creating a unique space for free thought and debate.

In the 17th century, beer went beyond the city gates and became a major product of maritime trade. During this period, experiments began with adding large amounts of hops so that the drink could withstand long voyages, thus laying the foundation for the styles of "traveling" beers whose descendants we value so much in the craft world today.

It was during this period that beer was finally established as the foundation of urban culture. The aroma of beer and the cheerful buzz emanating from the taverns became a symbol of the city's freedom and vitality.
