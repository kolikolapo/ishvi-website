18th Century - Science in Beer.

March 6, 2026

In the 18th century, brewing entered a new stage. Centuries of accumulated experience slowly integrated with science. It was during this period that instruments emerged that radically changed beer quality.

The use of the thermometer allowed for precise temperature control during the brewing process. Fermentation was no longer just a matter of intuition and experience; it became a measurable and repeatable process. Each stage now had a specific temperature and time.

The hydrometer further solidified this revolution. With its help, brewers began to predetermine sugar content, alcohol percentage, and final results. For the first time in history, beer became predictable.

It is from this period that the protocol of modern breweries took shape: control, stability, and quality. Beer was no longer just a craft—it turned into a technological process where the outcome was no longer dependent on chance. The scientific approach turned beer into a product whose character was pre-conceived and designed. This precision became the foundation upon which the modern craft world stands today—where every indicator deserves maximum attention.

The 18th century gave beer precision. And precision gave it quality.
