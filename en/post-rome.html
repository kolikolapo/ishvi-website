1st Century - Romans and Beer.

February 4, 2026

In the Roman Empire, as in ancient Greece, wine was the dominant beverage. It was the central part of the elite and civic life. Beer, from a Roman perspective, was often considered a simpler, more rustic drink.

However, Roman legionnaires actively consumed beer, especially in the northern provinces where grapevines did not thrive. In the Germanic lands, beer quickly merged with the culture of local tribes. Roman soldiers adopted local brewing methods, improved them, and spread them further. This was the beginning of the formation of beer's cultural diversity.

Although the Roman elite often looked down upon beer, the famous historian Tacitus described a beverage made from barley and wheat by the Germanic tribes that resembled wine. In Rome, it was called Cerevisia (in honor of Ceres, the goddess of agriculture), highlighting its deep connection to the earth and the harvest.

Archaeological discoveries in Britain, specifically at the Vindolanda fort, have confirmed that beer was not a luxury for legionnaires, but an everyday necessity. Letters have been found where soldiers request more beer supplies. This proves that professional brewers existed near the empire's borders, working specifically for Roman garrisons and thus laying the foundation for the first established breweries in Europe.

Beer travels from one civilization to another, adapting to local soil, climate, and people. It is no longer just the drink of one culture—it becomes part of a shared European history!
